# How Do You React to Feedback?

This question is about self-awareness and emotional maturity, not the feedback itself. The interviewer wants to know whether you can take criticism without getting defensive, whether you actually act on it, and whether you stay coachable as you grow. They are quietly screening out people who argue, sulk, or nod politely and then change nothing.

<br><br>

## Answer

> I welcome feedback as an opportunity to improve. I listen carefully, analyze it objectively, and apply it to enhance my performance.
>
>
> I try not to take it personally, even when it is critical, because the goal is better work rather than being right. Once I understand the point, I follow up to make sure the change actually holds, so the same feedback does not come up twice.

<br><br>

## Key Points to Hit

- **Openness:** You treat feedback as a chance to grow, not as an attack.
- **Composure:** You stay calm and non defensive, even when the criticism is tough.
- **Objectivity:** You separate the message from the emotion and judge it on merit.
- **Action:** You actually apply the feedback and follow through on the change.

<br><br>

## What to Avoid

- Do not sound defensive or hint that you rarely need correction.
- Do not claim you accept all feedback blindly, show that you evaluate it first.
- Do not give a vague answer with no sign that you act on what you hear.
- Do not bring up a grudge about past feedback or the manager who gave it.

<mark>Show that feedback changes your behavior, not just your mood.</mark>

<br><br>

## Other Ways This Question Can Be Asked

The same question often shows up with different wording, so recognize it in any of these forms:

- How do you handle criticism?
- Tell me about a time you received difficult feedback.
- How do you respond when someone points out a mistake?
- What do you do with feedback you disagree with?
- How do you take constructive criticism from a manager or a peer?
- Describe how you turn feedback into improvement.
