# Walk Me Through Yourself

This is usually the very first question, and it sets the tone for the whole interview. The interviewer is not asking for your life story, they want a tight professional snapshot (about 60 to 90 seconds) that shows who you are, what you have done that matters for this role, and why you are sitting in front of them. It also tests how clearly you communicate and how well you prioritize, since you have to decide what is worth saying in under two minutes.

## Key Points to Hit

- **Clear opening:** Your name, role, and years of experience in the first sentence.
- **Relevant journey:** Only the experience that builds toward this role, in order.
- **Proof with numbers:** One or two concrete results, not a list of adjectives.
- **Common thread:** One line that ties your story together (here, automating slow, error prone work).
- **Forward link:** End on what you want next and why this role fits it.

<br><br>

## What to Avoid

- Do not recite your resume line by line, the interviewer already has it.
- Do not go personal (childhood, hometown, hobbies) unless they ask.
- Do not run past two minutes, it signals you cannot prioritize.
- Do not list every tool and framework you have touched.
- Do not end abruptly, always close by connecting yourself to this role.

<mark>Tell a story with a direction, not a list of jobs.</mark>

## Other Ways This Question Can Be Asked

The same question often shows up with different wording, so recognize it in any of these forms:

- Tell me about yourself.
- Walk me through your resume.
- Introduce yourself.
- Give me a quick overview of your background.
- How did you get to where you are today?
- What should I know about you that is not on your resume?
